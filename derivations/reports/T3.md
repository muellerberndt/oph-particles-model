## Item 2. The no-go theorem (net chirality of an arrow block)

1. **Theorem, EXACT (degree theory).** Let the block symbol U(p) = P_chi W(p) P_chi of a constant projector that
   commutes with the tick be a continuous map T^3 -> SU(2). Nodes at phase 0 are the points with U = 1, at phase pi
   those with U = -1; write U(p* + q) = exp(-i eps0) exp(-i (J q).sigma) + O(q^2) and chirality = sign det J. Then
   the sum of chiralities at phase 0 = the sum at phase pi = deg U. (Proof: preimages of the two poles of S^3 with
   local orientation; at the south pole the positively oriented chart is -a for U = a0 - i a.sigma, which is the
   sign convention above.)
2. **Theorem, EXACT.** If the tick has finite range (the entries of W(p) are Laurent polynomials in e^(i p_j); every
   finite-depth circuit of local gates, every schedule, every unit cell, every finite product of ticks), then
   deg U = 0 for every such block. Proof for words of single-direction letters (the routing, all members of item 1):
   each letter depends on one linear form of p, so tr(g^-1 dg)^3 = 0, and W3[gh] = W3[g] + W3[h] because the cross
   term is d tr(g^-1 dg ^ dh h^-1). General proof: K_1 of the Laurent ring C[Z^3] is C^x + Z^3 (IMPORTED: fundamental
   theorem of algebraic K-theory for regular rings, Bass-Heller-Swan), so every unitary finite-range symbol is, after
   adding identity blocks, a product of elementary matrices (each joined to 1) and a diagonal monomial matrix, whose
   W3 is 0; the block symbol is itself finite range. Hence W3 = deg = 0.
3. **Answer.** No arrow block of any finite-range tick has net chirality over the zone, at phase 0 or at phase pi.
   A block with one chiral cone at phase 0 has a cone of the opposite chirality at phase 0 (not only at phase pi).
   Executed for CF-1, MEASURED Jacobians and EXACT signs (`item2.json`, `exact.json` E4): each block 32 nodes at
   phase 0 and 32 at phase pi, all with singular values 2, 2, 2 (per period), net chirality 0 at each phase; W3 of the
   block on 24^3 to 48^3 grids: 0.0. Per two-tick sublattice: phase 0 (0,0,0) +, (0,1/2,1/2) -, (1/2,0,1/2) +,
   (1/2,1/2,0) -; phase pi (0,0,1/2) +, (0,1/2,0) -, (1/2,0,0) +, (1/2,1/2,1/2) - (units of pi, block +1), equal to
   run A of CQ.
4. **What must be given up for a single chiral cone.**
   - Finite range. With exponentially decaying couplings the degree can be one, and the partner moves to phase pi
     (control, MEASURED: the smooth map U = (d0 - i d.sigma)/|d|, d0 = sum cos p_i - 2, d = sin p: one node at phase 0,
     seven at phase pi with net chirality +1, W3 = -0.950, -0.977, -0.987 at n = 24, 36, 48, i.e. |deg| = 1). With a
     discontinuous symbol (the audit's spectral clock) one node and no phase-pi node exist (control
     exp(-i 0.5 ptilde.sigma): couplings r |c(r)| = 0.64 to 0.65 for r = 4 to 128, a 1/r tail).
   - Not sufficient: translation invariance by one cell (a larger cell gives a finite-range symbol on a smaller torus,
     same theorem); the reflector form (the theorem holds for every unitary); a single clock (every finite product
     or power of finite-range ticks has finite range).
   - Outside the one-body theorem, IMPORTED and not checked here: a fourth dimension with the chiral cone on a
     boundary (domain wall), a momentum-dependent chirality with exponential tails (Ginsparg-Wilson/overlap), and
     multi-fermion gates that gap the mirror partners without breaking the gauge symmetry.
